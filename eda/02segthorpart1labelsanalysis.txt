Main finding: the aorta label is absent from all 20 GT files. Every patient contains
only labels 0, 1, 2, 3. 

Patient_07 is a bit of an outlier here - its GT2 file contains all five classes.
Heart and trachea are voxel-identical between GT and GT2. The esophagus in GT has
115229 voxels; in GT2 the esophagus has 25373 and the aorta 89856, which sum to
115229. The number of voxels that differ between the two files is exactly 89856.
It could be the case that GT is GT2 with every aorta voxel relabelled
as esophagus. GT2 might therefroe be the in fact true five-class annotation and GT is a version
with classes 1 and 4 merged. Note that the above is derived purely from voxel counts, and is a theory.

Nonetheless despite the uncertainty of this finding, it would be consistent with the "esophagus" volumes across all patients being 127 to 522 ml,
median 256 ml, which is many times more volume than that of a a real esophagus. What is
labelled esophagus in every patient is esophagus plus aorta. 

Everything else in the labels looks correct:
- Heart: everybody has one, essentially zero voxels below
  -500 HU, mean HU in the expected range. 
- Trachea: mean HU -650 to -895, 67 to 96 percent of voxels below
  -500 HU. 
- Esophagus and trachea end on the same slice in every patient, so the
  annotation was cut at a fixed anatomical level.

Intensity populations. Mean HU inside the heart mask is bimodal: about 95 to 110 in
patients 02, 03, 06, 09, 16 and about 0 to 35 in the other fifteen. The first group
must have been scanned with intravenous contrast. Windowing and normalisation choices have to
work for both, and the validation split should contain at least one of each.

For our next steps I would keep in mind:
1. Training on the current GT with K=5 would teach the network that the aorta never
   exists and that the esophagus is abnormally large. If the test set is annotated with five classes, the
   group would lose one whole class.
2. It could be the case that SEGTHOR_CLEAN is meant to be our de-coupled aorta and esophagus

3. We should consider our seperation options while we wait for part 2 of the data which might ship with corrected
   labels.
4. The five contrast patients and the three volume outliers should be spread acrossain and validation, not concentrated in one.
5. The two-group and spacing-outlier observations from the metadata still stand; the
   label analysis did not find any correlation between acquisition group and label
   quality.
